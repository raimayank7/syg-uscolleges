The colleges of New Jersey in brief

Of the 86 colleges listed for New Jersey, 33 are public, 44 are private nonprofit and 9 are private for-profit. By level, 49 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 34 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 3 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 297,450 undergraduates, 81% of them at public colleges. The three largest, Rutgers University-New Brunswick, Montclair State University and Rowan University, hold 24% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,776 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Lakewood (18), Jersey City (4) and Newark (4).

What college costs in New Jersey

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $6,660 a year for students from New Jersey and $10,620 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $12,500. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Warren County Community College ($2,746) and Pillar College ($2,785) and the highest at Jersey College ($56,587) and Chamberlain University-New Jersey ($42,922). The median net price across the state, $11,404, is 34% below the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

53 of the 86 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Princeton University (4.6% admitted), Talmudical Academy-New Jersey (43% admitted) and Yeshiva Toras Chaim (44% admitted). 35 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 28 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Princeton University (97%), Stevens Institute of Technology (89%) and The College of New Jersey (85%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 46%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Princeton University ($133,523), Stevens Institute of Technology ($118,358) and Chamberlain University-New Jersey ($96,897). The state median for that measure is $58,604, 10% above the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $18,586 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.
