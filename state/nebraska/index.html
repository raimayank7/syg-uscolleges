The colleges of Nebraska in brief

Of the 31 colleges listed for Nebraska, 14 are public, 15 are private nonprofit and 2 are private for-profit. By level, 21 mainly award bachelor's degrees and 10 mainly award two-year associate degrees. Together they teach about 78,725 undergraduates, 70% of them at public colleges. The three largest, University of Nebraska-Lincoln, University of Nebraska at Omaha and Bellevue University, hold 52% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 938 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Omaha (9) and Lincoln (5).

What college costs in Nebraska

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $6,078 a year for students from Nebraska. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $28,795. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Metropolitan Community College Area ($3,811) and Western Nebraska Community College ($5,979) and the highest at Creighton University ($32,456) and Bryan College of Health Sciences ($29,626). The median net price across the state, $16,214, is 7% below the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

16 of the 31 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Summit Christian College (42% admitted), College of Saint Mary (45% admitted) and York University (52% admitted). 11 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 12 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Creighton University (83%), Nebraska Methodist College of Nursing & Allied Health (76%) and University of Nebraska-Lincoln (69%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 55%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at University of Nebraska Medical Center ($81,009), Creighton University ($80,642) and Bryan College of Health Sciences ($73,322). The state median for that measure is $56,552, 6% above the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $19,750 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.

This site lists 31 open, degree-granting colleges and universities in Nebraska, out of 3,161 on the whole site. Schools that mainly give short certificates are not counted.

University of Nebraska-Lincoln in Lincoln, with about 19,178 undergraduate students.

Which college in Nebraska has the lowest net price?
