Reading this list

  • Net price is the full cost of a year (tuition, fees, books, living costs) minus grants and scholarships, averaged over students who got federal aid. For public colleges it counts students from inside the state.
  • International students and students from other states usually pay much more than the net price. Look at the published tuition and the college's own calculator.
  • A low price is only good value if students finish: compare the graduation rate.

Source: College Scorecard, U.S. Department of Education, data released on 2 November 2026; checked in November 2026. Net price, debt and earnings describe students who received US federal aid. Costs change each year. This is official data for general information, not advice: confirm costs and admission rules on the college's own website and at collegescorecard.ed.gov.
