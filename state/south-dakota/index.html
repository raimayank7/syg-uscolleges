The colleges of South Dakota in brief

Of the 20 colleges listed for South Dakota, 13 are public, 5 are private nonprofit and 2 are private for-profit. By level, 12 mainly award bachelor's degrees and 8 mainly award two-year associate degrees. Together they teach about 35,170 undergraduates, 84% of them at public colleges. The three largest, South Dakota State University, University of South Dakota and Southeast Technical College, hold 48% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,158 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Sioux Falls (5), Rapid City (3) and Mitchell (2).

What college costs in South Dakota

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $8,526 a year for students from South Dakota. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $34,540. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Oglala Lakota College ($1,431) and Sinte Gleska University ($4,047) and the highest at National American University-Rapid City ($31,654) and Dakota Wesleyan University ($24,039). The median net price across the state, $19,307, is 11% above the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

11 of the 20 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Mount Marty University (43% admitted), Augustana University (68% admitted) and Dakota Wesleyan University (73% admitted). 9 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 9 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Mitchell Technical College (79%), Lake Area Technical College (76%) and Augustana University (73%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 56%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at South Dakota School of Mines and Technology ($78,487), Augustana University ($61,271) and University of Sioux Falls ($59,404). The state median for that measure is $51,637, about the same as the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $21,802 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.

Questions about colleges in South Dakota

How many colleges are there in South Dakota?

This site lists 20 open, degree-granting colleges and universities in South Dakota, out of 3,161 on the whole site. Schools that mainly give short certificates are not counted.

What is the largest college in South Dakota?

South Dakota State University in Brookings, with about 9,198 undergraduate students.

Which college in South Dakota has the lowest net price?

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, Oglala Lakota College: students with federal aid paid an average of $1,431 a year after grants. Students from other states or countries usually pay more.
