The colleges of Michigan in brief

Of the 79 colleges listed for Michigan, 42 are public, 35 are private nonprofit and 2 are private for-profit. By level, 46 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 27 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 6 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 335,218 undergraduates, 88% of them at public colleges. The three largest, Michigan State University, University of Michigan-Ann Arbor and Grand Valley State University, hold 28% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,650 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Grand Rapids (7), Detroit (4) and Kalamazoo (4).

What college costs in Michigan

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $5,760 a year for students from Michigan and $11,563 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $35,530. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Kalamazoo Valley Community College ($2,461) and Alpena Community College ($3,238) and the highest at Lawrence Technological University ($33,466) and Arizona College of Nursing-Southfield ($32,556). The median net price across the state, $12,147, is 30% below the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

42 of the 79 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are University of Michigan-Ann Arbor (16% admitted), Hillsdale College (21% admitted) and Spring Arbor University (52% admitted). 31 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 29 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at University of Michigan-Ann Arbor (93%), Hillsdale College (89%) and Michigan State University (80%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 48%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Chamberlain University-Michigan ($96,897), Kettering University ($96,136) and University of Michigan-Ann Arbor ($92,832). The state median for that measure is $52,190, about the same as the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $19,500 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.

Questions about colleges in Michigan

How many colleges are there in Michigan?

This site lists 79 open, degree-granting colleges and universities in Michigan, out of 3,161 on the whole site. Schools that mainly give short certificates are not counted.

What is the largest college in Michigan?

Michigan State University in East Lansing, with about 40,922 undergraduate students.

Which college in Michigan has the lowest net price?

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, Kalamazoo Valley Community College: students with federal aid paid an average of $2,461 a year after grants. Students from other states or countries usually pay more.
