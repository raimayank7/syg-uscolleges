South University-High Point: cost, admission and results

Getting in

South University-High Point has an open admission policy: it does not turn applicants away on grades or test scores, though some courses can have their own requirements.

The net price is what students who got federal aid paid on average for a year after grants and scholarships were taken off. It is 12% above the median net price of colleges in North Carolina on this site ($16,597). International students normally pay the published tuition and do not receive US federal aid.

The students

39% of undergraduates receive a Pell Grant, the federal grant for students from lower-income families. 60% take a federal student loan. The undergraduate body is 87% women and 13% men. By undergraduate numbers it is number 91 of the 95 colleges in North Carolina on this site that report a size; the middle-sized one has 1,276.

What is not reported

The College Scorecard has no figure for an admission rate, the share of students who return for a second year or a graduation rate at South University-High Point. That is common for colleges that are small or new, that teach mostly graduate students, or that take few first-time, full-time students, because several measures count only that group. It says nothing by itself about quality. Ask the college directly for the figures that matter to you.
