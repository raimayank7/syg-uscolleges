The colleges of Kansas in brief

Of the 40 colleges listed for Kansas, 13 are public, 25 are private nonprofit and 2 are private for-profit. By level, 28 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 10 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 2 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 103,069 undergraduates, 85% of them at public colleges. The three largest, University of Kansas, Kansas State University and Johnson County Community College, hold 46% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 860 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Overland Park (5), Wichita (5) and McPherson (2).

What college costs in Kansas

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $6,111 a year for students from Kansas and $10,256 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $35,842. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Independence Community College ($2,571) and Allen County Community College ($3,011) and the highest at Bethel College-North Newton ($31,503) and MidAmerica Nazarene University ($30,947). The median net price across the state, $19,034, is 10% above the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

25 of the 40 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Sterling College (48% admitted), Barclay College (51% admitted) and Central Christian College of Kansas (53% admitted). 18 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 12 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Kansas State University (71%), University of Kansas (70%) and Hesston College (68%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 48%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at MidAmerica Nazarene University ($70,804), Baker University ($69,658) and University of Kansas ($67,963). The state median for that measure is $55,478, 4% above the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $21,750 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.

Questions about colleges in Kansas

How many colleges are there in Kansas?

This site lists 40 open, degree-granting colleges and universities in Kansas, out of 3,161 on the whole site. Schools that mainly give short certificates are not counted.

What is the largest college in Kansas?

University of Kansas in Lawrence, with about 21,217 undergraduate students.

Which college in Kansas has the lowest net price?

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, Independence Community College: students with federal aid paid an average of $2,571 a year after grants. Students from other states or countries usually pay more.
