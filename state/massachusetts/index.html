The colleges of Massachusetts in brief

Of the 96 colleges listed for Massachusetts, 30 are public, 65 are private nonprofit and 1 is private for-profit. By level, 70 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 19 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 7 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 305,266 undergraduates, 47% of them at public colleges. The three largest, University of Massachusetts-Amherst, Boston University and Northeastern University, hold 19% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 2,300 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Boston (22), Worcester (7) and Springfield (5).

What college costs in Massachusetts

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $7,755 a year for students from Massachusetts and $13,059 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $52,420. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Massachusetts Bay Community College ($1,756) and Springfield Technical Community College ($2,020) and the highest at The New England Conservatory of Music ($50,840) and Emerson College ($49,215). The median net price across the state, $23,444, is 35% above the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

63 of the 96 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Harvard University (3.6% admitted), Massachusetts Institute of Technology (4.5% admitted) and Northeastern University (5.2% admitted). 33 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 20 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Cambridge College (100%), Harvard University (97%) and Franklin W Olin College of Engineering (96%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 63%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Massachusetts Institute of Technology ($152,553), Babson College ($138,950) and Franklin W Olin College of Engineering ($138,832). The state median for that measure is $65,950, 24% above the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $22,089 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.

Questions about colleges in Massachusetts

How many colleges are there in Massachusetts?

What is the largest college in Massachusetts?

University of Massachusetts-Amherst in Amherst, with about 23,671 undergraduate students.

Which college in Massachusetts has the lowest net price?

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, Massachusetts Bay Community College: students with federal aid paid an average of $1,756 a year after grants. Students from other states or countries usually pay more.
