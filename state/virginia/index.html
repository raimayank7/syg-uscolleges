The colleges of Virginia in brief

Of the 80 colleges listed for Virginia, 23 are public, 41 are private nonprofit and 16 are private for-profit. By level, 54 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 18 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 8 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 339,647 undergraduates, 69% of them at public colleges. The three largest, Liberty University, Northern Virginia Community College and Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University, hold 34% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,217 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Richmond (6), Virginia Beach (6) and Lynchburg (5).

What college costs in Virginia

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $12,952 a year for students from Virginia and $28,010 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $31,551. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Patrick & Henry Community College ($3,885) and Paul D Camp Community College ($4,676) and the highest at Chamberlain University-Virginia ($44,710) and University of Richmond ($34,256). The median net price across the state, $22,186, is 28% above the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

47 of the 80 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Washington University of Science and Technology (7.3% admitted), Riverside College of Health Sciences (13% admitted) and Washington and Lee University (14% admitted). 32 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 18 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Washington and Lee University (96%), University of Virginia-Main Campus (94%) and William & Mary (88%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 52%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Washington and Lee University ($102,966), Chamberlain University-Virginia ($96,897) and University of Virginia-Main Campus ($96,468). The state median for that measure is $56,693, 7% above the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $21,843 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.
