The colleges of Iowa in brief

Of the 45 colleges listed for Iowa, 13 are public and 32 are private nonprofit. By level, 32 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 11 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 2 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 111,678 undergraduates, 70% of them at public colleges. The three largest, Iowa State University, University of Iowa and Kirkwood Community College, hold 49% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,227 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Dubuque (4), Sioux City (3) and Cedar Rapids (3).

What college costs in Iowa

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $7,050 a year for students from Iowa and $8,096 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $38,236. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Marshalltown Community College ($7,234) and Iowa Lakes Community College ($10,553) and the highest at Drake University ($29,415) and Briar Cliff University ($27,472). The median net price across the state, $21,412, is 23% above the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

29 of the 45 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Grinnell College (15% admitted), William Penn University (52% admitted) and Drake University (64% admitted). 23 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 12 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Grinnell College (86%), Iowa State University (77%) and University of Iowa (76%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 54%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Drake University ($78,855), Grinnell College ($72,106) and University of Iowa ($70,727). The state median for that measure is $57,656, 9% above the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $21,381 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.

Questions about colleges in Iowa

How many colleges are there in Iowa?

This site lists 45 open, degree-granting colleges and universities in Iowa, out of 3,161 on the whole site. Schools that mainly give short certificates are not counted.

What is the largest college in Iowa?

Iowa State University in Ames, with about 25,367 undergraduate students.

Which college in Iowa has the lowest net price?

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, Marshalltown Community College: students with federal aid paid an average of $7,234 a year after grants. Students from other states or countries usually pay more.
