The colleges of Ohio in brief

Of the 123 colleges listed for Ohio, 49 are public, 65 are private nonprofit and 9 are private for-profit. By level, 76 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 41 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 6 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 326,947 undergraduates, 72% of them at public colleges. The three largest, Ohio State University-Main Campus, University of Cincinnati-Main Campus and Ohio University-Main Campus, hold 29% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,006 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Cincinnati (10), Columbus (10) and Cleveland (4).

What college costs in Ohio

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $7,716 a year for students from Ohio and $17,960 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $36,598. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Lakeland Community College ($3,765) and North Central State College ($5,896) and the highest at Case Western Reserve University ($44,622) and Oberlin College ($41,987). The median net price across the state, $17,802, is about the same as the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

70 of the 123 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Denison University (17% admitted), Kenyon College (31% admitted) and Oberlin College (34% admitted). 47 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 45 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Case Western Reserve University (87%), Ohio State University-Main Campus (87%) and Kenyon College (86%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 47%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Case Western Reserve University ($89,865), University of Dayton ($83,775) and Ohio Northern University ($82,743). The state median for that measure is $55,997, 5% above the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $21,500 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.
