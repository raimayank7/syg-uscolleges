The colleges of Mississippi in brief

Of the 32 colleges listed for Mississippi, 22 are public, 9 are private nonprofit and 1 is private for-profit. By level, 17 mainly award bachelor's degrees and 15 mainly award two-year associate degrees. Together they teach about 119,700 undergraduates, 92% of them at public colleges. The three largest, University of Mississippi, Mississippi State University and University of Southern Mississippi, hold 42% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 2,178 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Jackson (4) and Hattiesburg (2).

What college costs in Mississippi

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $4,630 a year for students from Mississippi and $7,425 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $15,900. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Mississippi Delta Community College ($36) and Itawamba Community College ($2,729) and the highest at Jackson State University ($34,119) and Blue Mountain Christian University ($26,306). The median net price across the state, $11,006, is 37% below the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

16 of the 32 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Mississippi Christian University (29% admitted), Millsaps College (43% admitted) and Alcorn State University (45% admitted). 9 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 15 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at University of Mississippi (70%), Mississippi State University (69%) and Mississippi Christian University (64%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 49%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Millsaps College ($65,503), University of Mississippi ($59,112) and Mississippi State University ($54,296). The state median for that measure is $38,159, 28% below the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $18,930 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.
