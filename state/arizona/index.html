The colleges of Arizona in brief

Of the 39 colleges listed for Arizona, 8 are public, 14 are private nonprofit and 17 are private for-profit. By level, 22 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 12 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 5 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 394,853 undergraduates, 53% of them at public colleges. The three largest, University of Phoenix-Arizona, Grand Canyon University and Arizona State University Campus Immersion, hold 57% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,165 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Phoenix (9), Tempe (6) and Chandler (4).

What college costs in Arizona

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $11,967 a year for students from Arizona and $13,016 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $16,000. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Tohono O'odham Community College ($4,180) and Dine College ($6,944) and the highest at Pima Medical Institute-Mesa ($43,308) and Chamberlain University-Arizona ($41,882). The median net price across the state, $21,486, is 24% above the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

14 of the 39 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Arizona State University Digital Immersion (67% admitted), Arizona Christian University (71% admitted) and Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University-Prescott (77% admitted). 13 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 17 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Arizona College of Nursing-Tempe (83%), Universal Technical Institute of Arizona Inc (75%) and University of Arizona (71%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 38%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Chamberlain University-Arizona ($96,897), Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University-Prescott ($92,069) and Arizona State University Campus Immersion ($69,111). The state median for that measure is $44,446, 16% below the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $19,873 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.
