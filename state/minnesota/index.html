The colleges of Minnesota in brief

Of the 67 colleges listed for Minnesota, 30 are public, 33 are private nonprofit and 4 are private for-profit. By level, 40 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 21 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 6 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 191,383 undergraduates, 63% of them at public colleges. The three largest, University of Minnesota-Twin Cities, Capella University and Minnesota State University-Mankato, hold 32% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,878 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Saint Paul (10), Minneapolis (8) and Bloomington (4).

What college costs in Minnesota

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $6,628 a year for students from Minnesota. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $36,063. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Red Lake Nation College ($6,366) and University of Minnesota-Morris ($10,989) and the highest at University of St Thomas ($31,532) and Bethel University ($30,995). The median net price across the state, $19,467, is 12% above the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

36 of the 67 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Carleton College (20% admitted), Crown College (23% admitted) and Macalester College (29% admitted). 25 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 25 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Walden University (100%), Macalester College (91%) and Carleton College (89%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 55%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Carleton College ($82,742), University of St Thomas ($82,322) and Saint Johns University ($79,644). The state median for that measure is $55,601, 5% above the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $19,106 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.
