The colleges of West Virginia in brief

Of the 35 colleges listed for West Virginia, 21 are public, 9 are private nonprofit and 5 are private for-profit. By level, 20 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 13 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 2 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 96,302 undergraduates, 50% of them at public colleges. The three largest, American Public University System, West Virginia University and Marshall University, hold 67% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,014 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Huntington (3), Charles Town (2) and Fairmont (2).

What college costs in West Virginia

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $7,468 a year for students from West Virginia and $13,488 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $31,815. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at New River Community and Technical College ($1,743) and West Virginia Northern Community College ($4,488) and the highest at University of Charleston ($23,793) and Bethany College ($22,129). The median net price across the state, $11,123, is 36% below the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

15 of the 35 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are West Virginia University Institute of Technology (37% admitted), Bethany College (59% admitted) and University of Charleston (62% admitted). 11 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 18 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at West Virginia Junior College-Charleston (84%), West Virginia Junior College-Morgantown (77%) and West Virginia University (66%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 44%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Wheeling University ($64,655), Potomac State College of West Virginia University ($62,910) and West Virginia University ($62,910). The state median for that measure is $43,879, 17% below the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $18,978 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.
