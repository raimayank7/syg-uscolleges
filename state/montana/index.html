The colleges of Montana in brief

Of the 22 colleges listed for Montana, 17 are public and 5 are private nonprofit. By level, 10 mainly award bachelor's degrees and 12 mainly award two-year associate degrees. Together they teach about 35,573 undergraduates, 92% of them at public colleges. The three largest, Montana State University, The University of Montana and Montana State University Billings, hold 69% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 582 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Billings (3), Helena (2) and Great Falls (2).

What college costs in Montana

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $5,024 a year for students from Montana and $12,117 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $34,177. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Salish Kootenai College ($2,049) and Flathead Valley Community College ($9,911) and the highest at Carroll College ($26,295) and Montana State University ($23,106). The median net price across the state, $11,016, is 37% below the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

7 of the 22 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are University of Providence (50% admitted), Rocky Mountain College (70% admitted) and Carroll College (71% admitted). 5 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 14 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Carroll College (68%), Montana Technological University (61%) and Montana State University (59%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 42%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Carroll College ($66,371), Montana State University ($59,536) and Montana Technological University ($59,491). The state median for that measure is $45,792, 14% below the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $15,789 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.

Questions about colleges in Montana

How many colleges are there in Montana?

This site lists 22 open, degree-granting colleges and universities in Montana, out of 3,161 on the whole site. Schools that mainly give short certificates are not counted.

What is the largest college in Montana?

Montana State University in Bozeman, with about 14,451 undergraduate students.

Which college in Montana has the lowest net price?

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, Salish Kootenai College: students with federal aid paid an average of $2,049 a year after grants. Students from other states or countries usually pay more.
