The colleges of Louisiana in brief

Of the 35 colleges listed for Louisiana, 19 are public, 13 are private nonprofit and 3 are private for-profit. By level, 29 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 5 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 1 mainly awards graduate degrees. Together they teach about 130,158 undergraduates, 85% of them at public colleges. The three largest, Louisiana State University and Agricultural & Mechanical College, University of Louisiana at Lafayette and Southeastern Louisiana University, hold 40% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 2,032 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are New Orleans (11), Baton Rouge (6) and Shreveport (4).

What college costs in Louisiana

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $9,064 a year for students from Louisiana and $15,722 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $23,111. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Louisiana State University-Eunice ($7,454) and University of New Orleans ($7,761) and the highest at Tulane University of Louisiana ($40,693) and Chamberlain University-Louisiana ($36,976). The median net price across the state, $14,924, is 14% below the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

25 of the 35 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Tulane University of Louisiana (14% admitted), Southern University and A & M College (35% admitted) and Louisiana Culinary Institute (38% admitted). 17 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 6 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Tulane University of Louisiana (87%), Louisiana State University and Agricultural & Mechanical College (68%) and Centenary College of Louisiana (62%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 49%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Chamberlain University-Louisiana ($96,897), Louisiana State University Health Sciences Center-New Orleans ($84,003) and ITI Technical College ($71,551). The state median for that measure is $50,152, 6% below the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $22,246 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.

Questions about colleges in Louisiana

How many colleges are there in Louisiana?

This site lists 35 open, degree-granting colleges and universities in Louisiana, out of 3,161 on the whole site. Schools that mainly give short certificates are not counted.

What is the largest college in Louisiana?

Louisiana State University and Agricultural & Mechanical College in Baton Rouge, with about 30,594 undergraduate students.

Which college in Louisiana has the lowest net price?

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, Louisiana State University-Eunice: students with federal aid paid an average of $7,454 a year after grants. Students from other states or countries usually pay more.
