The colleges of Delaware in brief

Of the 6 colleges listed for Delaware, 3 are public, 2 are private nonprofit and 1 is private for-profit. By level, 5 mainly award bachelor's degrees and 1 mainly awards two-year associate degrees. Together they teach about 43,094 undergraduates, 80% of them at public colleges. The three largest, University of Delaware, Delaware Technical Community College-Terry and Wilmington University, hold 88% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 6,152 undergraduates. With so few colleges, each can be named: University of Delaware in Newark is public, with about 19,071 undergraduates and published tuition and fees of $17,660 a year. Delaware Technical Community College-Terry in Dover is public, with about 10,867 undergraduates and published tuition and fees of $5,445 a year. Wilmington University in New Castle is private nonprofit, with about 7,906 undergraduates and published tuition and fees of $12,930 a year. Delaware State University in Dover is public, with about 4,398 undergraduates and published tuition and fees of $11,214 a year. Goldey-Beacom College in Wilmington is private nonprofit, with about 691 undergraduates and published tuition and fees of $17,160 a year. Strayer University-Delaware in Wilmington is private for-profit, with about 161 undergraduates and published tuition and fees of $13,920 a year.

What college costs in Delaware

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $11,214 a year for students from Delaware and $21,204 for students from elsewhere. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Delaware Technical Community College-Terry ($8,401) and University of Delaware ($16,337) and the highest at Wilmington University ($20,210) and Delaware State University ($19,832). The median net price across the state, $17,045, is about the same as the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

3 of the 6 colleges report an admission rate. 3 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at University of Delaware (78%), Goldey-Beacom College (58%) and Delaware State University (42%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 42%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at University of Delaware ($83,800), Wilmington University ($60,391) and Goldey-Beacom College ($56,160). The state median for that measure is $54,012, about the same as the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $22,916 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.
