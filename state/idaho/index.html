The colleges of Idaho in brief

Of the 15 colleges listed for Idaho, 8 are public, 4 are private nonprofit and 3 are private for-profit. By level, 10 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 4 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 1 mainly awards graduate degrees. Together they teach about 53,069 undergraduates, 94% of them at public colleges. The three largest, Boise State University, University of Idaho and Idaho State University, hold 62% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 1,932 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Boise (3), Idaho Falls (2) and Nampa (2).

What college costs in Idaho

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $5,865 a year for students from Idaho and $15,839 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $28,595. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at College of Eastern Idaho ($5,384) and North Idaho College ($5,914) and the highest at Eagle Gate College-Boise Campus ($32,637) and Northwest Nazarene University ($25,262). The median net price across the state, $15,321, is 12% below the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

7 of the 15 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are The College of Idaho (49% admitted), Northwest Nazarene University (65% admitted) and University of Idaho (76% admitted). 5 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 7 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at The College of Idaho (68%), Boise State University (61%) and University of Idaho (60%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 48%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at Northwest Nazarene University ($60,370), Boise State University ($60,091) and University of Idaho ($58,095). The state median for that measure is $46,214, 13% below the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $18,875 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.

Questions about colleges in Idaho

How many colleges are there in Idaho?

This site lists 15 open, degree-granting colleges and universities in Idaho, out of 3,161 on the whole site. Schools that mainly give short certificates are not counted.

What is the largest college in Idaho?

Boise State University in Boise, with about 17,674 undergraduate students.

Which college in Idaho has the lowest net price?

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, College of Eastern Idaho: students with federal aid paid an average of $5,384 a year after grants. Students from other states or countries usually pay more.
