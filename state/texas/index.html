The colleges of Texas in brief

Of the 177 colleges listed for Texas, 85 are public, 65 are private nonprofit and 27 are private for-profit. By level, 101 mainly award bachelor's degrees, 63 mainly award two-year associate degrees and 13 mainly award graduate degrees. Together they teach about 1,129,317 undergraduates, 90% of them at public colleges. The three largest, Texas A&M University-College Station, Lone Star College System and Dallas College, hold 13% of the total, and the middle-sized college has 2,253 undergraduates. The towns with the most colleges are Houston (22), Dallas (16) and San Antonio (16).

What college costs in Texas

At the public colleges, the median published tuition and fees are $4,920 a year for students from Texas and $10,271 for students from elsewhere. At the private nonprofit colleges the median is $26,709. Published prices are not what most families pay. Counting colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the lowest average net price after grants is at Texas A & M International University ($336) and College of the Mainland ($447) and the highest at University of Dallas ($68,064) and West Coast University-Texas ($52,439). The median net price across the state, $14,222, is 18% below the national median of $17,357.

Getting in

81 of the 177 colleges report an admission rate. The hardest to get into are Rice University (8.0% admitted), Dallas Christian College (22% admitted) and Trinity University (26% admitted). 52 of them admit at least seven applicants in ten. 72 colleges have an open admission policy and report no rate: they do not turn applicants away on grades or test scores.

Finishing and earning

Among colleges with at least 300 undergraduates, the highest share of students finishing their course is at Rice University (95%), The University of Texas at Austin (89%) and College of Biblical Studies-Houston (86%). The median graduation rate of the state's colleges is 41%, against 49% for the country. Ten years after starting, former students with federal aid earn the most at West Coast University-Texas ($104,344), The University of Texas Medical Branch at Galveston ($98,687) and The University of Texas Health Science Center at Houston ($97,747). The state median for that measure is $51,026, 4% below the national $53,132. Students who finish leave with a median federal loan debt of $18,657 (national median $19,994). Earnings follow the subjects a college teaches as much as its quality, so compare like with like.
